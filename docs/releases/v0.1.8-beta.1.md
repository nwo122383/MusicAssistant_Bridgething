# Music Assistant BridgeThing 0.1.8 Beta 1

This is an **opt-in prerelease**, not a stable store update. The normal
BridgeThing catalog remains on **0.1.7**. Startup time and cellular audio still
need more driving tests; do not expect guaranteed sub-minute car-entry startup.

## Downloads

- `music-assistant-bridgething-v0.1.8-beta.1.zip`: import into BridgeThing as an
  app update. This is not Car Thing firmware. The existing app UUID is retained.
- `carthing-phone-player-v0.2.0-beta.1.apk`: optional native Android phone speaker.
  Android 12/API 31 or newer is required. Install on the phone, not the Car Thing.
- `SHA256SUMS.txt`: checksums for the two downloads.

No PC relay, custom BridgeThing firmware, or additional server component is
required. The APK is unnecessary when only controlling existing home speakers.
Keep existing app data and Bluetooth pairings when updating; uninstalling or
clearing the phone player can discard its stable speaker identity.

## What Changed

- **HA browser sign-in:** the controller's phone settings now offer Sign in to
  Home Assistant with PKCE, verified callbacks, and renewable credentials.
  Existing manual HA tokens remain supported under Advanced access token.
- **Connection recovery:** real-server health checks, isolated stale responses,
  nonoverlapping retries, and gateway polling help recover after the phone's
  BridgeThing link returns without reopening the Car Thing app. Settings echoes
  and inactive fields do not reset a working connection.
- **Phone-player startup:** HA mode can verify and load the selected native phone
  and its own queue before loading the wider home-speaker list. An unavailable
  selected player never silently falls back to a home speaker.
- **Remote artwork:** authenticated HA/MA image routes and a narrowly scoped
  SiriusXM artwork-host correction improve phone-backed artwork without
  disabling certificate checks or sending credentials to external image hosts.
- **Standalone phone audio:** the optional APK now owns the official native
  Sendspin engine rather than relying on the official MA Android app. It reuses
  one encrypted, persistent speaker identity across reconnects and APK updates.
- **Screen-off lifetime:** structured playback state keeps CPU protection during
  playback, including buffered audio across reconnects. Nonplaying buffering and
  recovery get a bounded wake window; retries cannot renew it indefinitely.
- **Control-link independence:** temporary Car Thing presence loss does not stop
  actively playing phone audio. Idle departure still releases the receiver and
  CPU wake lock after its grace period. Newer Android callbacks have version
  guards for the existing Android 12 minimum.

## Home Speakers Only

Install the ZIP and keep the existing selected speaker and connection path.
The phone-player APK and its login are not needed for Sonos or other existing
MA outputs. The stable release's library, AI Radio, groups, per-member volume,
presets, voice requests, and fullscreen controls remain available.

## One-Time Setup For Phone Audio

1. Pair/connect the Car Thing in the normal BridgeThing Android app, then import
   this beta ZIP. BridgeThing firmware/libbridgething 0.17.1 or newer is required.
2. In our app's phone settings, choose **Home Assistant / Nabu Casa**, enter the
   remote HA HTTPS URL, and choose **Sign in to Home Assistant**. Use an HA
   account, not Nabu Casa billing credentials. Advanced HA tokens still work.
3. Install the APK, open **CarThing Phone Player**, enter the same remote HA
   address, and sign in there once as well. These are separate authorizations;
   the helper does not read BridgeThing's private credentials.
4. Grant Nearby Devices permission and, preferably, notifications. Select the
   intended Car Thing and choose **Enable automatic startup**, then approve
   Android's companion-device association.
5. In the Car Thing app's Players view, choose **CarThing Phone** and assign the
   smartphone volume control. The selected phone remains selected while offline.
6. Connect the phone's normal media output to the car stereo's Bluetooth/AUX
   adapter. The phone receives the music; the Car Thing provides the controls.

The intended daily flow does not require opening multiple phone screens. Forced
stops, revoked permissions, or Android background restrictions can prevent that
automatic flow; reopening the helper after a force stop may be necessary.

## Connection Requirements And Limits

- The helper uses an existing **MA app/add-on with Home Assistant ingress** and
  an HA remote HTTPS connection. HA Core without MA add-on ingress is not covered
  by this native audio path. HA add-on discovery requires appropriate HA access.
- **The helper does not implement MA Remote-ID/WebRTC login/audio yet.** The
  controller's WebRTC connection remains available, but an MA Remote ID alone
  does not configure the APK's audio connection.
- HA and MA credentials are not interchangeable. Browser sign-in avoids manually
  creating tokens; no passwords are stored by our app. Stored renewable tokens
  and device exports must still be treated as sensitive.
- Stock BridgeThing Bluetooth paging/retry delays remain unresolved. This beta
  does not replace BridgeThing's Bluetooth implementation or guarantee one-minute
  startup after power-on or returning to range.
- Brief audio underruns remain under investigation. Cellular handoffs, actual
  car Bluetooth audio routing, and longer locked-phone drives need more testing.
- SiriusXM may supply a station logo instead of the current song's album cover.
- To return to stable 0.1.7 after beta HA browser sign-in, configure a manual HA
  token again: the older ZIP does not understand the serialized OAuth session.
  Keep the phone helper's data; do not uninstall it as a downgrade workaround.

## Verification

The release is built from the tested development changes. All **257 webapp
tests**, the native helper's **26 unit tests**, and the **302 pinned Sendspin
engine tests** passed. The ZIP and non-debuggable APK built successfully. Android lint
completed with zero errors and 15 warnings; warnings are not hidden by a baseline.

A seven-minute simulated-battery cellular run kept the phone screen off, the
foreground receiver and CPU protection active, audio frame counters advancing,
and the controller connected in every sample. The raw audio-underrun counter
increased near the end, so this is not a completely dropout-free audio claim.
During a separate simulated departure, active audio survived beyond the old
shutdown threshold. After an intentional pause, the idle receiver stopped at
the 17-second observation; simulated return restored the same speaker in about
five seconds. That is not a real Bluetooth cold-start timing.

Saved login, selected speaker, association, and player identity were preserved.
